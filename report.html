1. Motivation: who has the problem

I am the sole official distributor of General Hydroponics in Thailand. The business sells more than US$1M of hydroponic nutrients a year (General Hydroponics, Athena, Advanced Nutrients, Grotek and others) to grow shops, farms and resellers. Stock arrives in a container from Los Angeles about every two months, so the lead time is about 60 days. I approve and decide; sales and warehouse staff take orders.

Today there is no system. Each customer has their own WeChat group. An order is a chat message such as 三色（63折）绿色粉5：120*3=360 …: Chinese nicknames (紫粉, 鸦片粉, 根水), bag colours, Thai Baht, US dollars, RMB and USDT in the same thread, and discounts written as 5折 / 七折 / 63折. I copy each order by hand into a Word "sales report"; stock is whatever I remember plus an occasional count.

Why it matters. A late reorder costs two months of sales because the next container is 60 days away. A regular customer who stops ordering is noticed months later, if ever. And hand arithmetic leaks money: while building the app we found three real errors in my own records (Section 6).

The owner had not looked at any of these ("我们之前没有这样的想法") — the honest baseline is the Word file. What this app does differently:

  1. Reads the orders the business actually gets: mixed-language chat text with nicknames, bag colours, currencies and discounts. A 44-SKU catalog maps every spelling and nickname (confirmed by the owner with product photos) to one standard SKU; an unknown or ambiguous name stops instead of being guessed.
  2. Human approval before any write, with roles: staff submit, only the owner approves, every step is in an audit log.
  3. Deterministic math: reorder dates and dormant-customer flags come from formulas in code; the LLM only reads text and explains results.
  4. Real business, real data: 5 real container purchase sheets, the real 24 Oct stocktake and 41 real orders from June–October 2026. A synthetic dataset on the same real catalog ships with the repo.

Honest limits of the comparison: an ERP does invoicing, multi-warehouse and accounting, which this app does not.

3. The agents

AgentRoleTools (all read-only)
IntakeOne order message → structured OrderDraft (product words, qty, unit price, currency, discount, stated subtotals, questions, suspicious sentences)lookup_product (catalog)
OrchestratorOwner's Q&A front desk; routes to the specialists (agents as tools) and combines answersask_inventory_agent, ask_crm_agent
InventoryStock, sales, what to reorder and by whenget_reorder_report, get_stock, get_sales_summary
CRMDormant customers, customer history, follow-up WeChat messagesget_dormant_customers, get_customer_history

Code, not agents, does everything that must be right: redaction before the model; re-checking every SKU against the catalog; line and total arithmetic; floor price; stock; quantity limits; injection phrases; the approval, the write, and the audit log. No agent has a write tool.

Model choice. All four agents use gpt-6-luna through the course Portkey key (Responses API, function tools, structured output). The work is extraction and short explanations, not deep reasoning, so a small cheap model is enough; the measured intake cost is $0.00039 per order. Separate small agents keep each prompt short and each tool set minimal (the intake agent cannot even see the reorder tools), at the cost of more requests per question (Section 4).

One traced run, step by step (a real order from 5 Oct 2026)

  1. Staff pastes the WeChat order on the 录单 page and picks the customer (the name stays on this computer): 三色（63折） 绿色粉5：120*3=360 黑色粉15：120*15=1800 红色粉：10*120=1200 Big bud：600 番茄水：220 …
  2. Redaction removes phones, addresses, links and bank accounts and replaces known customer names with codes.
  3. Intake agent (2 model requests, 6,149 input tokens of which 2,844 cached, $0.00091) calls lookup_product for each line: 绿色粉 → FP-GROW-25LB, 黑色粉 → FP-CAMICRO-25LB, 番茄水 → BOTANICARE-CALMAG-PLUS-6GAL; applies the 63折 group discount; keeps the quantity written next to the name (5).
  4. Code validation recomputes every line: "绿色粉: 5 × 120 = 600, the order says 360" — the customer was charged for 3 bags but receives 5. It also flags prices below the floor price and the stock check.
  5. Pending queue: the record waits for the owner with yellow warnings and red blocking issues. Staff cannot approve.
  6. Owner approves → code writes the sale, moves stock, and writes approve + write audit entries.

Cost-cutting steps and their trade-offs

  • Prompt caching: the intake agent's instructions and the 44-SKU catalog form a fixed ~5k-token prefix with a fixed prompt_cache_key. Measured cache hit rate 91%: $0.00039 per order instead of $0.00090 (−56%). Trade-off: the Q&A prompts are shorter than the 1,024-token minimum, so they get no caching (0% measured).
  • Deterministic tools instead of LLM math: reorder and churn are SQL + Python; tool results are compact JSON, so a reorder question costs $0.00059 with no risk of invented numbers.
  • Small specialists: each agent sees only its own tools and prompt. Trade-off: the orchestrator adds requests (4.2 per question on average) versus one big agent; we accept it for clearer tools and safety.
  • Usage limits on every run (intake 6 requests; specialists 5 requests / 6 tool calls; orchestrator 4 / 3) cap the worst case.

The provider's content filter is a fourth layer.
Data leaks (customer names, phones, addresses)Redaction before every model call; agents only ever see customer codes; real data, .env and the real database are gitignored; private examples were scrubbed from the git history.

Wrong actions (bad quantity, price, stock)Line and total arithmetic, floor price, quantity > 500, quantity > stock → warning or block; owner-only approval; audit log.
Infinite loops / overspendingRequest and tool-call limits on every agent run.

Time and money (sources stated)

  • Order entry: owner's baseline ≈ 2 min per order by hand into Word; with the app the parse takes about 6–13 seconds (measured in the UI) and the owner reviews a pre-filled card. The bigger gain is that the record is now structured: stock, history and reorder come for free.
  • Reconciliation and stock checks: ≈ 3 h / week by the owner's estimate; stock is now the stocktake plus every approved movement, so "how many do we have" is one click. Estimate: most of those hours saved.
  • Stockouts: the owner estimates about US$30,000 of business lost to stockouts in the last three months. With a 60-day lead time the reorder page shows the latest order date per product before the shelf is empty. Even avoiding one-tenth of that is US$3,000 — against a model bill of about $0.05 per user per month.
  • Lost customers: 7–8 regular customers disappeared in two years without anyone noticing. The CRM agent lists customers who are silent for more than twice their usual gap, with their usual products and a draft message.
  • Errors: 3 arithmetic/recording errors worth about US$920 found in 4 months of the owner's own records (above).

7. Limits and next steps

  • History is incomplete: container files from Feb–Nov 2025 are lost, and 33 historical orders have no customer recorded, so customer analytics on real data starts thin; new orders are recorded with the customer from now on.
  • Reorder needs approved sales: daily rates come only from approved orders; until the backlog is approved some products show "no recent sales". No seasonality, MOQ, container capacity or cash-flow limits yet.
  • Text only: orders are pasted as text; reading WeChat screenshots and invoice photos directly is the next step, then a direct WeChat/LINE connection.
  • Local, single warehouse: next is a hosted version with staff logins, phone UI and backups, and an export to the accountant.
  • Fixed exchange rates (taken from the owner's own conversions) should become daily rates.
